# Blueprint 1 - Training Compatibility Evidence Notes

## Scope
- Goal: add recent training context to Blueprint 1 and surface cautious guidance for treatment timing before or after exercise.
- Important framing:
  - the literature does **not** support strong universal incompatibilities between PBM and common exercise modes;
  - evidence is mixed, highly protocol-dependent, and stronger for localized musculoskeletal recovery than for global wellness or performance claims;
  - the product should therefore communicate:
    - `generally compatible`
    - `compatible with caution`
    - `limited evidence`
    - or `wait until recovered`
  - instead of claiming hard contraindications unless those are already part of the treatment safety notes.

## Main source set used

1. Functional performance meta-analysis  
   PMID 38150056  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38150056/  
   Key takeaway:
   - PBMT may help fatigue recovery in healthy individuals.
   - No consistent evidence for strength or functional-capacity enhancement.

2. Exercise oxidative stress systematic review / meta-analysis  
   PMID 36139746  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/36139746/  
   Key takeaway:
   - Low-to-moderate certainty evidence supports reduced oxidative damage and increased antioxidant activity after exercise.
   - Useful mainly for recovery-oriented framing, not for broad ergogenic claims.

3. Whole-body PBM and exercise performance / recovery review  
   PMID 39883205  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39883205/  
   Key takeaway:
   - Whole-body PBM may help sleep quality.
   - No convincing evidence of benefit for exercise performance or recovery.

4. PBM during a multiweek training program in trained individuals  
   PMID 35151026  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35151026/  
   Key takeaway:
   - Adding PBM during a training program did not produce additional benefits in trained individuals.
   - Important caution against overclaiming adaptation benefits.

5. Pre-exercise cycling trial with null result  
   PMID 33519511  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33519511/  
   Key takeaway:
   - PBMT 30 min or 6 h before severe-intensity cycling did not improve time to exhaustion or exercise physiology in healthy untrained men.

6. Pre-exercise shoulder resistance trial with positive fatigue result  
   PMID 38015822  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38015822/  
   Key takeaway:
   - One localized preconditioning trial reported attenuated shoulder fatigue before strenuous resistance exercise.
   - This supports only cautious, localized, exercise-specific use.

7. Pilates + PBMT chronic low back pain trial  
   PMID 39057559  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39057559/  
   Key takeaway:
   - Mat Pilates improved outcomes, but PBMT added no additional measurable effect.
   - Supports a `limited evidence` label around yoga / Pilates synergy claims.

8. High-intensity intermittent BJJ lower-limb trial  
   PMID 38288386  
   https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38288386/  
   Key takeaway:
   - No dose-dependent ergogenic effect was observed in a high-intensity intermittent anaerobic protocol.

## Product interpretation adopted

### HIIT
- Before treatment:
  - evidence mixed and mostly insufficient for strong benefit claims.
- After treatment:
  - localized recovery-oriented use is generally plausible after the initial cool-down period.
  - do not present as guaranteed performance or fatigue enhancement.

### Cardio
- Before treatment:
  - mixed / limited evidence.
- After treatment:
  - generally compatible for recovery-oriented treatment use.
  - evidence is stronger for post-exercise redox / fatigue modulation than for endurance enhancement.

### Upper / lower body strength
- Before treatment:
  - some localized trials suggest reduced fatigue.
  - still should be shown as optional and evidence-mixed.
- After treatment:
  - reasonable recovery-oriented use, especially for musculoskeletal targets overlapping the trained area.
  - no solid basis to promise better long-term adaptation.

### Yoga / Pilates
- No convincing incompatibility found.
- Direct timing evidence is scarce.
- When PBMT was combined with Pilates in chronic low back pain, no clear additive effect was shown.

## Practical UX rule
- If the user trained very recently and the guidance profile is `compatible with caution`, the app can recommend a short cool-down / settle-down gap before treatment.
- This waiting gap is a product safety / comfort heuristic, not a hard scientific contraindication threshold.
